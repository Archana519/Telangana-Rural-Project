\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:} Planning internal access routes for rural villages is usually manual, and detection studies seldom connect building extraction to route planning. \textbf{Methods:} We present a framework that derives building and facility targets from 0.6~m imagery with an extended DS-UNet and StrongDINOv2Seg, builds an ordinal 10~m cost surface from land cover and slope with hard constraints, and plans a shared network in two stages in which routes may end on roads or on other routes. The case study is Damarakunta village, Siddipet district, Telangana, India. \textbf{Results:} For 163 house targets, independent least-cost routes total 6753~m of unique length, whereas the proposed network needs 5069~m, a reduction of 24.9\%. All 168 targets connect on the road-line network and no hard constraint is violated. Under synthetic omission and addition of targets the saving remains between 24.9\% and 26.6\%. Keeping routes out of detected buildings lengthens the network by about 22\%. Detection agreement with an independent building layer is 72\%, and no field validation exists. \textbf{Conclusions:} Shared-network planning on a cost surface can substantially shorten rural access networks, but the results are planning alternatives and require labelled buildings and field verification.
\end{abstract}
